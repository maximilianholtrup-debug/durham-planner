THE IDEAS BEHIND EVOKE

The power of a word.

A guide to Roberto Assagioli’s The Technique of Evocative Words and how to practise it with Evoke.

In his essay, psychiatrist Roberto Assagioli proposes that words do more than name a quality: they can also call up ideas, images, feelings and possible ways of acting associated with it. CALM, for example, becomes a focus for reflection on what calmness means and feels like to you.

Three principles described in the text

01

Ideas influence us

An idea or image may lead towards related mental states, bodily states and actions.

02

Attention reinforces

Paying attention to a quality and revisiting it may deepen its influence.

03

Some effects are unnoticed

Assagioli suggests that associations can work without conscious awareness.

The method is to deliberately choose constructive words and bring them into view, rather than relying only on whatever impressions the environment provides. The original practice uses simple word cards placed where they will be seen.

About these claims: This describes Assagioli’s theoretical framework, not a guarantee that looking at a word will change how you feel or behave. Explore the practice at your own pace.
Source: Roberto Assagioli, M.D., The Technique of Evocative Words, “The Effectiveness of Words” and “Methods of Using Words” (the supplied PDF, pp. 1–2).

The original text offers a flexible exercise rather than a rigid set of rules. Here is a practical sequence drawn from its recommendations:

1
Choose a quality

Pick a word you want to cultivate (e.g. COURAGE or PATIENCE), or draw one at random as your word for the day.

2
Keep it visible

Put the card where you notice it, such as a desk or bedside. In the app, you can revisit or print your chosen word.

3
Relax and observe for 1–2 minutes

Look attentively at the word. Allow related thoughts, images or memories to arise without forcing them.

4
Reflect and record

Consider what the word means to you. Make notes about the ideas or images that emerged.

5
Experience the quality

Explore what it might feel like to embody that quality in your own life.

6
Reinforce it if helpful

Optionally say the word aloud, murmur it or write it repeatedly. Try the same word for a week or month, or rotate daily.

Important: Assagioli cautions that even apparently positive words can provoke negative reactions. You can choose another word, pause or stop if the practice feels upsetting. This app is for reflection, not treatment.
Source: Assagioli, The Technique of Evocative Words, “Methods of Using Words”, “Different Methods of Application”, and “A Word of Caution” (pp. 2 and 4).
